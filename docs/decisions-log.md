|---|---|---|

| 2026-10-07 | Color palette = Fern (green → white soft gradient, light default + dark toggle) — *superseded by golden hour palette* | Chosen from green-to-white options; avoids the brownish cast of the earlier Matcha (green + butter) gradient |
| 2026-10-07 | Typography = Newsreader headings + Manrope body — *body superseded by Schibsted Grotesk* | Bookish feel that stays clean and readable at small sizes and in dark mode |
| 2026-10-07 | Home order: hero → projects → tech stack → experience → beyond code → contact — *superseded 2026-10-08* | Projects are the strongest evidence right after the hero; the stack section backs them up instead of leading with generic logos |
| 2026-10-08 | Saved baseline visual: golden hour scene, blurred then pixelated (~4px pixels, heavy blur), sunset palette, crisp Newsreader + Manrope type on top; sky shifts to dusk then night on scroll | Pixel structure with a soft, moody, dreamy picture, in the spirit of Drew's background but warm. Avoids the smooth "AI gradient" look. |
| 2026-10-08 | Texture = animated film grain, faint at golden hour and building toward night. Backup: hybrid (dither at golden hour → grain at night). — *backup retired 2026-10-11: grain only* | Grain gives the nostalgic, filmic, dreamy mood; keeping it light when the sky is brightest avoids it feeling busy. Rejected: pixel shimmer (read as low resolution in motion); dither alone (busy at night). |
| 2026-10-08 | Direction = clean layout + rich atmosphere; journal metaphor as subtle touches only | Literal journal props (tickets, stamps, tape) read as tacky; what's consistently liked (Drew's site, the papercraft reference) is an artful, textured backdrop under a simple, readable layout |
| 2026-10-08 | Central metaphor = Golden hour (the site is one evening, sunset into night) | Came from personal reflection: love of sunsets where warm light meets the incoming night, plus nostalgia for old photos. Gives the abstract backdrop a meaning and resolves light vs. dark. Rejected first: torn-paper journal, peeling layers, topographic map, constellation, stage formation. |
| 2026-10-08 | Accent = text shifts Starlight → Sun gold as the sky darkens; Sun gold four-point sparkle and link underlines throughout. Saved alternatives: First star, Honey, Apricot. | Warm colors look right but are hard to read on the bright golden hour sky; cool colors read well but clash. Shifting puts each color where it reads best and makes the accent "set" with the sun. The sparkle doubles as the first star. Rejected: greens (too dark to read), Twilight (hard to read), backed chips (extra UI chrome). |
| 2026-10-08 | Keep Newsreader for headings; hero name stacked with italic surname, 72px, weight 400, letter-spacing -0.045em, line-height ~0.95 | The font wasn't the problem, the default spacing was: at display size it looked loose. Tightening the letters makes the name read as a polished wordmark. Compared: Source Serif 4, Spectral, Crimson Pro, EB Garamond, Libre Caslon, Noto Serif Display. Rejected earlier: Fraunces and Instrument Serif (curves too much), Literata (not clean or mature enough). |
| 2026-10-08 | Body font = Schibsted Grotesk (replaces Manrope) | Wanted something with the feel of Anthropic Sans (custom and not licensed for outside use): a quiet, editorial grotesk beside a bookish serif. Schibsted was built for a news publisher, so it's confident and editorial, and it holds up on the bright sky. Compared: Manrope, Inter, DM Sans, Figtree, Geist, Hanken Grotesk, Instrument Sans, Plus Jakarta Sans, Inter Tight, Host Grotesk, Albert Sans. Paid option if ever needed: Söhne (Klim). |
| 2026-10-08 | Hero focuses on one thing: the name section. No photo, or at most a small portrait (A2-style). No medium or large images on the landing view. | A visitor's first moment should land on a single thing. A bigger image competes with the name and is too much on arrival. Rejected: A1 tilted film print, B2 photobooth strip, C2 large framed photo. |
| 2026-10-08 | Hero layout = editorial left with a small round portrait above the name; supporting text and nav quieted | The left vignette was made for this placement, so the name sits in the calmest, most readable part of the sky. The sun and horizon on the right frame the name instead of competing with it. The small portrait reads like a signature, not a second focal point. Rejected: B centered (text sits on the sun's glow, hardest to read at golden hour), C title card (name too low, reads like end credits). |
| 2026-10-08 | Home order: hero → intro → tech stack → experience → projects → beyond code → fun / experimental / interactive → connect | An introduction right under the hero lets visitors meet the person before the work. Tech stack and experience set up the projects. Beyond code and playful, interactive pieces sit near the end as a reward before connect. Rejected: GitHub activity section (graph is too sparse right now and would work against the impression). Replaces the earlier projects-first order. |
| 2026-10-08 | Sky modes: scroll (default), pick the time (slider or light/dark switch), or local time | Makes the sky something visitors can play with instead of only watch, which fits "animation responds to the visitor." Light/dark becomes part of the metaphor (sun up vs. night) instead of a generic theme toggle. Local time makes the site feel live and personal to each visitor. |
| 2026-10-08 | Full 24-hour sky cycle; mode 2 uses a circular time dial plus light/dark buttons; local time is a toggle; modes 2 and 3 stay fixed | A day mode needs new skies anyway, so finish the whole cycle. A circular dial matches how time actually loops, and the buttons give the instant light/dark people expect. Fixed means the visitor owns their sky, so connect doesn't force stars. |
| 2026-10-08 | Day theme = E "navy morning haze": navy ink (#1F1A3A), soft cream glow, wide cream haze on the left (~0.72 strength, fading by ~70% across), plum accent (#4E3270) | Navy reads cleanly on the daytime blues and passed contrast at every daytime hour. The wider haze reads like morning mist and keeps the bright feel. Rejected: C cream text with plum haze (dims the whole left side, so light mode stops feeling light). Not chosen: A navy with light haze, B plum ink, D deep sea ink. |
| 2026-10-09 | Tech stack details: logo + name in soft outlined pills; heading left; rows wide (96px margin to margin) and centered; pause on hover | Logos scan faster than words. Pills keep each item readable where the rows cross the sun. Wide centered rows give the section its own centered feel while their left edge still lines up with the heading. Pausing on hover lets people stop and read. Rejected: text only, logo + name without pills, full-bleed rows, contained left rows (less presence), contained centered rows (edges misaligned with a left heading), fully centered section. |
| 2026-10-09 | Tech stack = simple looping rows like Drew's site (small label, heading, one-line description, two slowly looping rows); accepted as an exception to "animation responds to the visitor," like the cloud drift | Simple, familiar, and lets people read the stack while it sits centered on screen. Rejected: rows that only move with page scroll (to see more items you'd have to scroll the page, which pushes the stack off-center or off-screen). |
| 2026-10-09 | Vignette widened ~25% everywhere, including the hero | Needed for the intro's facts row. Since the background is fixed, one width site-wide is simpler to build, and the hero still looked right with it in sunset, light, and dark. Rejected: widening only behind the intro (more build work for no visible gain). |
| 2026-10-09 | No site-wide "text on the left, sky on the right" rule | Jared doesn't want layout locked that way. Each section decides its own placement, with readability checked case by case. |
| 2026-10-09 | Intro quick facts = a row of four short columns under the paragraph, with a slightly wider vignette | On the right, the facts sat on the sun at sunset and blended into the headland in light mode. A row keeps them left of the sun, reads like a caption strip, and keeps the section short. Rejected: stacked list under the paragraph (makes the section taller), right side with a soft panel (introduces the site's first box and covers part of the sun). |
| 2026-10-09 | Intro = lead + paragraph with a quick-facts list | Serves both audiences: recruiters scan the facts in seconds, everyone else reads the story. Rejected: single column (nothing for skimmers), small film prints (would steal the beyond-code section's photos), big statement (depends entirely on unwritten copy; revisit in the content pass). |
| 2026-10-09 | Scroll sky follows overall page progress; anchors: hero = golden hour, connect = full night | Sections don't need their own sky stage, and the cycle stretches to fit however long the page grows. Only the two moments that matter are pinned: the tuned hero and the stars at connect. Rejected: mapping each section to a sky stage. |
| 2026-10-08 | Return to default via a "sunset" pill that appears in the nav only when the visitor has left sunset mode; the dial (location TBD) will also include it | Naming the mode makes it clear sunset is the site's real default, not a broken dark mode. Rejected: three-state cycling button (unclear), clicking the active mode again (people expect only light and dark, so they'd be confused why "light" isn't light, and the way back is hidden). |
| 2026-10-08 | Planning doc split into a main hub page + subpages (visual system, discovery, formal design doc, decisions log), with steering notes for Claude on the hub | One long page was slow to re-read every session. The hub holds principles and current state; details load only when a task needs them. |
| 2026-10-09 | Build stack = Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript, Tailwind CSS + CSS variables for sky-driven colors, Canvas 2D sky, Motion for drag/UI transitions, content as TypeScript data files + MDX, hosted on Vercel. Rebuild from scratch in the existing repo; build in VS Code with Claude Code, one step at a time. | Sky mode is one piece of state that recolors nearly everything, which a single React app shell handles well; Next.js also gives server routes for later widgets and continuity with the old site. CSS variables let the sky update colors every frame without re-rendering. Canvas 2D matches the prototype pipeline and is easy to tune. Content in the repo is simplest for a single editor. Rejected: Astro + React islands (sky state would have to cross isolated islands), SvelteKit (new framework mid-recruiting), WebGL shader (harder to tweak; revisit only if phones stutter), headless CMS (extra account and setup for one editor), Notion as CMS (slow, rate-limited API, expiring image links, site depends on Notion). |
| 2026-10-11 | Phone sky = option C, the recomposed tall coast (build step 3) | The approved prototype was landscape only (680×380), so a portrait phone needed its own composition rather than a cropped or squeezed desktop scene. Chosen from 2–3 compositions on the /lab page. Not chosen: the other /lab options. |
| 2026-10-11 | Pixel squares stay 4px on every screen, instead of the prototype's fixed 170×95 canvas | A fixed canvas would make the squares huge on big monitors and tiny on phones. A fixed square size keeps the soft-mosaic texture the same everywhere. |
| 2026-10-11 | Texture = film grain only; hybrid backup retired | Compared side by side in the real build and not chosen. Grain alone (lighter when bright) gives the filmic mood without the texture switching partway down the page. |
| 2026-10-11 | Reduce Motion: grain freezes on a single frame; the same rule will apply to stars and cloud drift. Scroll blending stays on. | Keeps the textured look for visitors who ask for less motion, without anything moving on its own. Scroll blending only moves when the visitor scrolls, so it doesn't count as unprompted motion. |
| 2026-10-11 | Vignette blends through the sky keyframes (each keyframe carries the vignette's color, strength and reach), not a separate CSS variable. All three evening keyframes use plum at 0.45. | The vignette changes with the sky, so it belongs in the same data. The day theme then only needs new keyframes, with no separate vignette logic. |
| 2026-10-11 | Build order: grain moved into step 3 (static sky); phone side margin 24px as a placeholder until the hero step | Grain is part of how the sky looks, so it's judged with the first sky. The margin only needs to be good enough to test readability for now. |
| 2026-10-11 | The accent eases (smoothstep) over the first third of the scroll; sky colors mix channel by channel (straight RGB) like the prototype | Easing keeps the shift gentle at both ends instead of snapping. Straight RGB keeps the approved prototype look; revisit if mid-dusk colors look muddy. |
| 2026-10-11 | Stars = single crisp pixels (one 4px square each) in the top ~two-thirds of the sky above the horizon, on desktop and the tall phone coast. Density is set per pixel (1 in 240 low-res pixels), so it's the same on every screen, and stars keep fixed positions across reloads. They fade in 0 → 0.3 → 1 (golden hour → dusk → night) and each twinkles. Cloud drift as in the prototype. | The prototype's fixed 46 stars would look ~5× sparser on a big screen, so density follows screen size. The prototype's density (1 in 170) read as too many on the full-size sky; ~30% fewer keeps a real night sky without crowding. Measuring the band from the horizon gives the phone's tall sky the same share of stars. Rejected: a fixed count of 46 (sparse on desktop), prototype density 1 in 170 (too many), shrinking the star area instead of thinning. |
| 2026-10-11 | Nav links = about · experience · projects · résumé · contact, lowercase, in page order | Matches the scroll order, and recruiters reach the résumé in one click. Rejected: the prototype's "Work · About · Résumé · Contact" (never decided, no experience link). |
| 2026-10-11 | Nav hides while scrolling down and returns on scroll up; on phones a "menu" button opens full-screen links over a darkened, blurred sky | Keeps the sky controls reachable anywhere without covering text while reading. The full-screen menu needs no box and reads best. Rejected: scrolling away with the hero (controls stranded at the top), always pinned (floats over text); phone dropdown (the site's first box), stacked links with no box (the bottom link sits on the bright cloud at golden hour). |
